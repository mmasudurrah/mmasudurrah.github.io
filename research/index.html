Research

I work on reinforcement learning and robot learning for surgical and medical robotics. The goal is semi-autonomous surgical systems that carry out parts of a procedure reliably and return control to the surgeon when a situation is beyond what they can handle. My work toward this goal is in three areas, summarized below with representative papers. The publications page has the complete list.

Reinforcement learning

Policy-gradient methods such as PPO lose policy entropy as training proceeds, which limits exploration, and their performance can depend on the choice of action distribution and on careful tuning. Robust Policy Optimization (RPO) addresses this by perturbing the parameters of the action distribution during training: a random value is added to the mean of the Gaussian policy, which keeps the policy’s entropy from collapsing. On continuous-control benchmarks from DeepMind Control, OpenAI Gym, PyBullet, and IsaacGym, RPO generally outperforms PPO and baselines based on entropy regularization, alternative action distributions, and data augmentation. RPO is included in the CleanRL and skrl libraries and is used in Physical Intelligence’s released implementation of real-time action chunking.

A second line of work concerns generalization. Image observations contain many features that are irrelevant to the task, and policies trained on them can fail when those features change. In work published in Findings of NAACL 2024, we convert image observations into natural-language descriptions and train the policy on the text; these policies generalized better than policies trained directly on images. Related papers on generalization and advantage estimation appeared at ECML-PKDD 2022 and ICMLA 2022.

Vision-language models for clinical decision support

Whether a burn needs surgery depends on its depth, which is difficult to judge by visual inspection; expert visual assessment is about 70% accurate. With clinical collaborators at a U.S. burn center, I develop methods that assess burns from ultrasound, including B-mode and Tissue Doppler Imaging. Labeled data in this setting is scarce, so these methods use pre-trained vision-language models together with first-order logic reasoning.

KODER (MIUA 2025, Best Paper Award) generates several diagnostic hypotheses from the images and clinical knowledge and checks them for consistency with an SMT solver. AURA (AAAI/IAAI 2026, oral) was evaluated on patient data collected over one year at the burn center and reached up to 93% accuracy on the surgical decision and 87% on fine-grained burn depth. AMBUSH-AI, an automated noninvasive burn diagnostic system, is published in Annals of Surgery (2026), and a related system that incorporates electronic medical records appeared in JMIR Medical Informatics (2025).

Semi-autonomous surgical robotics

Teleoperated surgical robots need low-latency, high-bandwidth communication, which is often unavailable in remote or austere settings. In SARTRES, the surgeon works at a standard teleoperation interface while the system recognizes the atomic sub-tasks being performed (surgemes) and the remote robot executes them semi-autonomously. On a peg-transfer task from the DESK dataset, surgeme recognition accuracy was 91.5% and execution success was 86%. DESERTS (ICRA 2021) applies the same approach under communication delay and maintained an 87% task success rate with delays of up to 5 seconds.

Related work includes the DESK dataset for surgical skill transfer (IROS 2019), skill transfer between robots and between procedures (RO-MAN 2019, 2021), and surgical activity recognition with logic constraints (RO-MAN 2021).

Current work

My current work brings these areas together in surgical tasks. I am studying how work should be divided between a robot and the surgical team, how learned policies behave when a procedure departs from the plan, and how to evaluate semi-autonomous systems against the disruptions that occur during real procedures. I am also working on integrating foundation models into reinforcement learning, supported by an NSF EAGER award.
