Md Masudur Rahman

Learning-based autonomy for surgery and other high-stakes settings.

I develop reinforcement learning and foundation-model methods for systems that act reliably when conditions change and recognize when to hand control back to a person, with surgical and medical robotics as the driving application.

I am a postdoctoral researcher in Purdue’s Edwardson School of Industrial Engineering, working with Juan P. Wachs. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Purdue in 2024, advised by Yexiang Xue.

Research agenda

When should an autonomous system act, and when should it hand control to a person?

In surgery, capability alone is not enough: a system must also recognize when a situation has moved beyond what it can handle. My research builds toward that in three connected parts: learning methods that stay reliable when conditions change, multimodal reasoning that grounds decisions in clinical evidence, and shared-control designs that divide work between the system and the clinician. Each is evaluated against the disruptions and failures that occur in real procedures.

The same questions apply to robot learning and decision systems beyond surgery, wherever errors are costly.
